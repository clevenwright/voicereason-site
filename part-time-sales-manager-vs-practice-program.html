Guide · Part-time manager or practice

Should I hire a part-time sales manager or use a practice-call program?

It depends on what is missing. A part-time sales manager brings judgment: reviewing real calls, helping you hire, and setting how your team sells. A practice-call program brings practice: your team can run the hard calls on their own time, as often as your plan's minutes allow, and every new hire gets the same practice. Some businesses use both, with the manager setting the plan and the practice program giving the team time to rehearse it.

What a part-time sales manager does better

A good part-time manager is a person with experience. That matters for work that needs judgment:

  • Real call reviews. They can listen to your team's real calls and tell you what is going wrong with your actual customers.
  • Hiring help. They can help you write the job, run interviews, and spot who will do well.
  • Setting the plan. They can look at your business and decide what your team should say, what to offer, and how to follow up.
  • Hard conversations. They can sit down with someone who is struggling and decide, with you, what happens next.

A practice program does not do these things. If you need someone to make decisions about people, plans, or pay, you need a person.

What a practice-call program does better

A part-time manager has limited hours. A practice-call program does not wait for a manager's calendar:

  • On their own time. Your team member can practice from a desk, from the truck between appointments, or from home.
  • The same practice for everyone. Every new hire runs the same customer and the same calls, so no one gets a lighter start.
  • Many tries. Your team member can run the same tough call again and again until it feels natural.
  • A record of every practice call. Every practice call and its report are on your Calls page, so you can see who is practicing and how each call went.

When to use both

The two fit together well. A common setup looks like this:

  1. The manager sets the plan. They write down how your team should handle first calls, price questions, and follow-ups.
  2. You add that plan as your playbook. Every report then measures the call against the way your team sells.
  3. Your team practices between visits. They run the calls the manager cares about, on their own time.
  4. You share the reports with the manager. Instead of starting from scratch each visit, they can go over who practiced, which calls they ran, and how each one went.

The manager's time goes to judgment, and practice happens between visits.

When one is enough

  • Start with a practice program if you already know how you want your team to sell and you mainly need them to get comfortable saying it.
  • Start with a part-time manager if you are not sure how your team should sell yet, or if your biggest problem is hiring the right people.
  • Use both if you are growing, hiring often, and do not have time to coach yourself.

Questions

Is the customer a real person?

No. The customer is an AI voice that sounds like a real person and stays in character.

Can a practice program replace a sales manager?

Not for everything. It gives your team practice and a written report after every call. It does not hire people, review real calls, or make decisions about your business. Those still need a person.

Can my part-time manager see the practice calls?

Yes, through you. Every practice call and its report are on your Calls page. Go over who is practicing, which calls they ran, and how each one went with your manager.
