Headline

Cold call opened with a product pitch and a Tuesday meeting ask, then stalled when asked what the plant does

Your coach says

The buyer gave you a real opening when they said "You've got the right person if this is about plant operations" and then asked what you knew about the site. That question was an invitation, not a trap, and it's the whole call. Before you dial a plant like this, spend five minutes learning what they make, and open warm and human: your name, your company, one honest reason you're calling, and a quick ask for thirty seconds. Then ask one grounded question about their world, let them name a pain, and only propose a meeting once they've told you something worth meeting about. Build the preparation underneath that and the next one goes very differently.

What you did well

  • You named your company up front: "I am with Acme Chemicals," so the buyer knew who was calling.
  • You gave a reason tied to their world: "a lot of manufacturing plants are being really thoughtful about the chemicals they use around their employees," which is a reasonable hook.
  • When the buyer stopped you cold, you didn't bulldoze past it or argue; you stayed in the conversation.

Work on these next

  • Open by earning the minute: give your name, your company, one plain reason you're calling this person, and ask for their time before proposing anything. Here the pitch and a specific meeting slot came out before any permission.
  • Do the homework before you dial. When the buyer asked "What kind of manufacturing do you think happens at this facility?" the answer was "I'm not really sure." For a facilities director who respects plain competence, that is the moment the call turned.
  • Lead with a question that lets the buyer name a problem, rather than proposing a walkthrough first. Nothing about their situation surfaced because the first move was the ask.
  • Close on a next step the buyer actually says back. "on Tuesday at 2 o'clock" was stated at them, not agreed to.
