The part nobody else does

Everyone hands your rep a scorecard. We give them the conversation.

We scanned this market in September and found more than fifteen tools selling AI practice calls. Every single one of them ends the same way: the call stops, and a written score appears. The rep reads it if they feel like it, usually later, usually once. A score tells a rep what happened. A debrief tells them what to do about it, at the only moment they still remember why they said what they said.

Headline

Strong build of the problem, then the price came out before the value did and the discount followed within a minute.

Summary

Dawson opened well and got the homeowner talking about the two cold rooms upstairs before touching equipment. The turn came at minute four: asked what it would run, he gave the number immediately and then filled the silence himself by offering to see what he could do on the price. The homeowner never had to ask. From there the conversation was about money rather than about the house, and the two competing quotes were never brought into the open.

Coaching

You are good at the part most people are bad at. You built the problem in the homeowner's own words and you did not rush it. Protect that. The habit to break is what you do with silence after you say a number: you discounted before anyone pushed back, which taught the homeowner that the price moves. Say the number, then stop talking. Let it sit. And when somebody tells you they have two other quotes, that is an invitation, not a threat. Ask what they liked about them.

Discovery Strong
  • Opened on the house rather than the equipment: "Which rooms never get comfortable?"
  • Let the homeowner describe the two cold upstairs bedrooms without interrupting, then repeated it back before moving on.
  • Established the system age, last service and that a previous repair had failed within a year.
Building value Developing
  • Connected the new system to the cold bedrooms, which was the right link to make.
  • Did not connect it to the failed repair or the money already spent on it, which the homeowner had raised twice.
  • Efficiency was described in equipment terms rather than in what the homeowner would notice.
Price and objections Needs work
  • Discounted unprompted, eleven seconds after stating the price, with no objection raised: "and I can probably see what I can do on that number."
  • The homeowner mentioned two other quotes twice. Neither was ever asked about.
  • When asked "why is yours higher?" the answer described the brand rather than the install.
Close and next step Developing
  • Asked for the business directly rather than trailing off, which many reps do not do.
  • Accepted "let me talk to my wife" without establishing when or what would decide it.
  • Left a written quote but no agreed date to follow up.

Did well

  • Spent the first four minutes on the house, not the equipment.
  • Repeated the homeowner's own words back before moving on.
  • Asked for the business plainly.

Work on next

  • Say the price, then stop talking. Do not fill the silence.
  • Ask about the competing quotes rather than pretending they are not there.
  • Tie the value to the failed repair, which is the thing that already cost them money.

How it works

Three steps, and none of them involve installing anything.

STEP 01

You issue a code

One access code per rep, from your account. Label it with their name and the address their feedback should go to. Add or remove seats whenever the team changes.

STEP 02

They call the number

From the truck, the driveway, a hotel room, a flip phone. They give the code and get a buyer who knows their customers and makes them earn it. Just the phone call itself.

STEP 03

The report writes itself

The rep gets their read by email, so every seat needs a rep email on file. You see every call and every transcript on your account, in order.

Questions

The things a manager actually asks.

What counts as a seat?

One seat is one person who makes practice calls, and one access code. Your own manager login does not consume a seat. If you have eight reps, you need eight seats.

Do my reps have to log in to anything?

No. That is the point. A rep never creates an account, never downloads anything and never needs a smartphone. They dial a number and give an access code. Their feedback arrives by email, at the address you give us when you set up their seat.

Is the buyer a real person?

No. The buyer is software using a synthetic voice, and so is the coach. Nobody is listening in, and nobody's customer is ever on the call.

Is the call recorded, and is that legal?

Yes it is recorded, and the first thing the line says on every call, before anything else, is that the call is being recorded. Nobody's customer is ever recorded, because this is practice and not a real appointment. The full detail is on the recording and consent page.

How is this different from the other practice tools?

Every tool in this category ends a call by handing over a written scorecard. We built the part that comes after: a coach that talks the call through with the rep while it is still fresh, instead of emailing them a score to read later.

Does the coach use up my practice minutes?

Yes. Coaching time uses the same minutes as the practice call and counts against the same 60 minute call limit on the Coach plan.

What does it score them on, and is that fair?

Discovery, qualification, how objections were handled, and whether the call ended with a real next step. Every coaching claim is checked against the transcript before the report is sent. It gives each rep their own read rather than a single number.

Can I cancel?

Yes, at any time. Canceling stops the next charge, and your subscription stays active through the end of the period you have already paid for.

How long does setup take?

You issue codes and send them out. There is nothing to integrate and no CRM to connect.

Do you train on our calls?

No. Your practice conversations belong to your account and are not used to train models for anyone else.
